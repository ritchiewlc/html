The Problem

After your program ends, what can you actually show?

You know your program works. But the proof was never designed to be caught. It lived in the room and left when everyone did.

Now it's a reflection buried in a Google Form, a screenshot lost in WhatsApp, a Miro board nobody opened again. When someone asks "what was the impact?", you type "improved confidence and collaboration skills" into a cell and move on.

That's not evidence. That's what people write when the real proof was never captured.

The Framework

Three questions. That's the whole thing.

One metric. One evidence. One testimonial. That's a proof unit.

The evidence01

Did they do it?

what they actually produced.

The metric02

Did it work?

what changed, what improved.

The testimonial03

Did they like it?

what they said about it.

If you can answer all three with real artifacts, you have proof. Or else, you have brochures.

The Promise

Your proof is already happening.You're just not catching it.

Every session, someone is building something real, changing something concrete, saying something that matters. All of it disappears when the room empties.

The report writes itself. The proof becomes your pitch. Your people own something they're proud of. And you finally know what's working, not from a guess, from real signal.
