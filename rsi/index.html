When AI builds itself.
Recursive self-improvement, in plain terms.

Anthropic just published the most concrete public accounting yet of how much of their own model development is now being done by their own models. The headline number: 80% of merged code is written by Claude. The deeper question: how long until Claude is also deciding which code to write next?

Anthropic Institute, When AI builds itself · May 2026 · Favaro & Clark

01What it actually is

A closed loop where AI builds the next AI.

Today humans run every step of model development. The question Anthropic is putting on the table: what happens when AI runs the loop end-to-end, and improves itself with each turn?

The destination
Recursive self-improvement: an AI system that can fully autonomously design, train, and deploy its own successor. Not inevitable. Possibly soon.

05The narrowing human role

Engineers don't disappear. They move up the stack.

Anthropic models a researcher's career as a ladder of "specification autonomy". Junior engineers execute tasks someone else defined. Senior engineers pick the method. Staff engineers pick the problem. The data shows Claude is climbing that ladder from the bottom.

Where Claude is today vs. where it isn't

Based on Anthropic's own framing of how work gets harder as you become more senior.

Junior: specified task
"The export button isn’t working. Please fix it."
Example from production: a routine upgrade began crashing tens of thousands of training jobs. An engineer pointed Claude at the live incident with cluster access. In ~2 hours, Claude isolated the obscure debugging flag, reproduced the crash, and confirmed a fix. Work that would normally take 2 to 3 days.
Senior: specified goal
"Investigate why the network slows down under heavy load."
Internal experiment: optimize training code for speed. Opus 4 in May 2025 averaged ~3×. Mythos Preview in April 2026 averaged ~52×. A skilled human needs 4 to 8 hours to reach 4×.
Staff: specified problem
"Can a weaker model reliably supervise a stronger one?"
In an open-ended AI safety research run, two human researchers over a week recovered ~23% of the gap between weak-supervisor and trained-on-correct-answers performance. Agents running for 800 hours on ~$18,000 of compute recovered 97%. Humans picked the problem and rubric. Agents designed every experiment.
Principal: pick the problem
"What should the team build next quarter?"
Anthropic judges how often the model picks a better next research step than the human does. On moments where the human's choice had room for improvement, the model’s suggestion was judged better 51% of the time in Nov 2025 (Opus 4.5), and 64% in April 2026 (Mythos Preview). Still early. The direction is clear.
06Three futures

What could happen next.

Anthropic lays out three scenarios. They are betting on the second, watching the third.

1

The curve bends. Capabilities plateau.

Today's exponential curves turn out to be S-curves. Returns to scale diminish, then flatten. Either AI hits a wall, or the binding constraint shifts to chips, energy, or interconnect.

Even at frozen capabilities, the world changes: Project Glasswing found over 10,000 critical software vulnerabilities in its first weeks. A 100-person company can increasingly do the work of a 1,000-person one.

Anthropic's bet: unlikely. Every measurable capability, including the "squishier" ones, has so far followed the same curve. They have not seen it bend.

Most time to adapt · Anthropic ranks this last
2

Compounding efficiency gains. Humans still set direction.

AI development becomes substantially automated, but humans continue choosing which experiments to run and judging the results. Each engineer and researcher becomes a force multiplier on a much larger pyramid of agents.

100-person companies could do the work of 10,000- or 100,000-person organizations. Knowledge work, government services, and influence operations all move at a new speed, in both directions.

The friction Anthropic already feels: Amdahl’s law. Speed up one part of the process and the bottleneck just moves. Human code review is already a bottleneck inside Anthropic. Spotting and clearing bottlenecks may become the most important organizational skill.

The trajectory the evidence supports
3

Full recursive self-improvement.

If the trend continues, and AI develops the judgment to set its own research direction, the pace of AI development becomes a function of compute alone. Humans shift almost entirely to oversight, validation, and verification of a virtual lab run by AI.

  • Embodied intelligence (robotics) likely follows the same path of increasing returns at decreasing cost.
  • More intelligence can still not learn what a drug does over decades of use, hold an election before a constitution says so, or turn a stranger into an old friend in a weekend. The felt pace of life is still set by the slowest bottleneck.
  • Alignment is the most uncertain variable. The rare misalignments of today could compound, growing more frequent and less understood, until we lose control of the systems building the systems.
  • If it works, this world unlocks Machines of Loving Grace at speed. If it does not, the cost is the one Dario sketched in The Adolescence of Technology.
Transformative · Anthropic is least certain here

The proposed response: a verifiable pause, not a unilateral one.

Anthropic argues the world would benefit from the option to slow frontier AI development, if it could be done in a way that slowed everyone equally and verifiably. A unilateral pause by one lab changes who is in front, not how fast the field moves.

The hard part: AI is uniquely hard to verify. Training runs are easier to conceal than missile silos. Inputs are general-purpose. The incentive to defect quietly is enormous, because the actor that continues while others pause inherits the lead. The closest historical analogue, nuclear arms control, took decades to build the infrastructure and the trust. AI may not have that long.

The Anthropic Institute says it will spend the coming months organizing conversations with policymakers, researchers, civil society, and other AI companies, then publish what comes out. The window to deliberate is now, and it is open to people outside the labs.
